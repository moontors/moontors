Moontors 0.8.4

Moontors, the agentic development environment (ADE) for Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and Grok Build. This release brings **Settings** and **Profile** with their tabs down the side, and the **Configuration** dialog, on cards; **Configure** for each agent from **Profile**, with no session; a new **Add MCP server**; a composer that keeps to one row; **Archived** read beside its list; Grok Build's context window; and an account switch that says what it waits for.

What's new:

- **Settings** and **Profile** have their tabs down the page's left side, each with its icon, or in a row over the page where it's narrow, and a tab slides in, from the right for one further along and from the left for one before it, or shows at once if you've asked your system for less motion. Each of Settings' tabs opens with its name and an ⓘ that says what it sets and whether it syncs, then its settings on a card, and a change that's refused says why under the tab's name. **New Session**'s agents and quick actions stand in two cards, side by side where the page has room, each agent by its logo, and the one **Create session** starts on is marked **Default**. See [Settings](https://moontors.com/docs/settings/#opening-it).
- **Profile**'s tabs each say what they hold at a glance: **Providers** how many of your agents are signed in, and **Account** your account's email, or **Not signed in**. **Account** stands on cards, each sign-in method by its provider's logo, or an envelope for your email and password. On **Providers**, each agent's state follows a dot: green signed in, grey checking at start, blue and glowing while it installs, red where its install failed, and hollow signed out or not installed, as in the Android app's **Accounts**. See [Your account](https://moontors.com/docs/moontors-account/#your-account) and [Providers](https://moontors.com/docs/sign-in/#providers).
- The **Configuration** dialog is on cards, in two columns where it has room, each with an ⓘ that says what it is and when a change to it applies, and a card on the agent's tab folds by its heading. A card's list stands about five entries tall, and a longer one scrolls within the card, the entry it cuts off fading out; past its end, the wheel or trackpad goes on to scroll the dialog. Once the agent's tab lists more than twelve entries in all, a **Filter** tops it, keeping the entries whose name, description, command, value or source holds what you type. **Status** marks the mode **(default)** where it's the default, as it marked the model, and shows your account on a card of its own, and **Current session** sums up what the session's agent works with: its added folders and rules, then its MCP servers, skills, plugins, hooks, apps, subagents, instruction files and its own lists, each with how many it holds or how many of them are on, a row opening its part. What the running agent says of itself is under **Reported by** and the agent's name. See [The Configuration dialog](https://moontors.com/docs/configuration/#the-configuration-dialog) and [`/status`](https://moontors.com/docs/insight-and-code-workflows/#status).
- On **Profile**'s **Providers**, each agent's card has a cog, **Configure**, which opens that agent's own setup with no session: your MCP servers, skills, plugins, hooks, subagents and instruction files, its own lists, and the servers you added for **All projects**, the same in every folder. A switch there reaches every session of that agent, a running one at once where it can, otherwise from its next turn, and a server you add there is for **All projects**. What a project's own files add, and what a folder's trust holds back, show only in a session's **Configuration**. Grok Build lists your setup through its own command line, so where Moontors hasn't installed it yet, **Configure** installs it first. See [From Profile](https://moontors.com/docs/configuration/#from-profile).
- **Add MCP server** is laid out anew. The transport is three choices: **HTTP**, where a new server starts, then **Local command**, then **SSE**, the older protocol. Beside the form, where there's room, a guide to the transport you chose gives common shapes of its command or URL. A field says what's wrong with it under it once you've typed in it and moved on, or when you press **Add server**, and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter on Windows and Linux) adds the server. Paste a server's settings anywhere in the form and it fills in, one server at a time, and a whole command line pasted into an empty **Command** splits into the command and its arguments. Once it's added, the agent's tab brings the new server into sight. See [Adding an MCP server](https://moontors.com/docs/configuration/#adding-an-mcp-server).
- The composer's toolbar stays on one row, on your computer and in the Android app. Where it has no room for every setting, the last ones move, from the end, into **More settings** (⋯), and come back as the composer widens; its menu has a line for each, with its icon, name and value, which opens the same choices as its button. The permission mode never moves: where even its words have no room, it shows as its icon alone. On your computer, they folded into one **Settings** menu before; in the Android app, fast mode, the output style and Daybreak each have their own button now, where they shared one. See [The composer](https://moontors.com/docs/sessions-composer/).
- **Archived** reads beside its list: click a session to read its transcript beside the list where the window has room, the newest until you choose another. The arrow keys move from row to row, the transcript following, Enter moves into the transcript, and Escape goes back to the list, then out of Archived; in a narrow window, the transcript shows alone, with **Back to the list**. The list groups its sessions under **Today**, **Yesterday**, **This week**, **This month** and **Older**, each row by its agent's logo, with a search over titles, projects and agents (never their transcripts' words), **All agents** where the archive holds more than one, and a count. **Reopen** is on each row, and **Delete** in its ⋯ menu. In the Android app, the archive groups and searches the same way, **Delete** is under a row's ⋯ or a long press, and where the screen holds two phone-wide sides, an archived session opens beside the list. See [Archiving and deleting](https://moontors.com/docs/archiving-and-deleting/).
- With Grok Build, **Context window** (`/context-window`) is a session setting where the session's model lists more than one, as Grok 4.7 does on a grok.com sign-in: 256K or 500K tokens, on your computer and in the Android app. It starts at the model's own window, marked **(default)**, and Grok Build takes a change at once, even mid-turn, the context ring counting against it from then on. Grok Build lists no windows on an API key, so it isn't offered there. See [Session settings](https://moontors.com/docs/configuration/#session-settings).
- When a sign-in to another account waits to switch a session, its header, on your computer and in the Android app, and **Configuration** say all the while which account it switches to and what it waits for: the turn under way, its background tasks, by their count, or other work the agent still runs. Background tasks, such as a wait for CI, hold the switch too, and the session switches as soon as the last one ends. While they hold it, **Switch now** asks first, naming the tasks it stops: **Keep waiting**, or **Stop and switch**, which stops them and switches, never cutting a turn. Signing in again to the account the session still runs on, before it has switched, calls the switch off, its background tasks still running; signing in to a third account switches to that one, waiting the same way. See [Providers](https://moontors.com/docs/sign-in/#providers).
- **Don't wait for me** is a switch in the mode picker, which stays open as you turn it, so you see it change, and while it's on, the mode's button shows a no-entry mark after the mode, on your computer and in the Android app. See [Don't wait for me](https://moontors.com/docs/permission-modes/#dont-wait-for-me).
- A model change's row in the transcript names the model and its effort, on your computer and in the Android app: one left at the model's own names the default it ran at, as Moontors knew it then, and a model that takes no effort names none. See [The model picker](https://moontors.com/docs/sessions-models/#the-model-picker).
- Once Moontors starts again, a Claude Code session's fast mode and output style are asked once more and show an ellipsis meanwhile; if Claude Code can't answer, they show what your Claude Code settings set, else fast mode off and the Default style, where a session restored or branched could show an ellipsis with nothing chosen. See [The model picker](https://moontors.com/docs/sessions-models/#the-model-picker).
- A newer version you open while the one before still works in the background, as after you install it yourself, takes over once the work under way ends, and until then a line under the header says a newer Moontors is ready and what it's waiting for. There's nothing to do: it goes by itself once the newer version has taken over. See [Updates](https://moontors.com/docs/updates/).
- On your computer, a transcript's rows keep to their places as a long transcript first opens and as a reply streams in, where for a moment one could be drawn over the next.
- On your computer, an agent's code block draws its four rounded corners whole, and its focus ring when Shift+Tab reaches it.
- A plugin your claude.ai account syncs to Claude Code is listed once in **Configuration**, where it could show twice.
- A screen reader lists **New Session**'s **Agents** and **Quick actions** under the tab's name in Settings, and an agent's usage limits on **Profile**'s **Providers** under the agent's name.
- On moontors.com, the footer and the [notices](https://moontors.com/policies/notices/) say the sign-in providers' names and logos belong to their owners, as the agents' and clouds' do.

This version updates the Android app; the iPhone app isn't updated this time. The Windows and Linux builds still aren't signed, so Windows asks once before Moontors opens. The README's "First launch" and "Updates" sections say what to expect on each platform.
